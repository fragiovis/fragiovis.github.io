Research

Mechanism-guided imputation of missing data

Apr – Sep 2026 M.Sc. thesis (110/110 cum laude) · University of Michigan with Prof. H.V. Jagadish, on site in Ann Arbor Jun – Sep 2026 · advisor at Roma Tre: Prof. Riccardo Torlone

Real-world data is often missing for a reason: people skip the questions they are afraid to answer. Standard imputation methods (MICE, PMM, generative imputers) assume the opposite. My thesis treats the missingness mechanism as a signal rather than a nuisance: it uses an expert's knowledge of why values are missing to reconstruct them under missing-not-at-random self-masking, together with a recommender that picks the right correction for each case. Evaluated on real survey data against MICE, PMM, NARFCS and GAIN, it reaches a 3–10× lower Wasserstein distance than MICE on CPS retirement income. A paper on MGI is in preparation for submission.
